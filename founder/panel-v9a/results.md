These are simulated buyers, not customers. Use this to find objections and weak spots, then confirm the big ones with real people before you spend.

## What would flip a no

- The free list of 15 companies actually naming buyers I don't already know in the target country, plus a reference I can phone at a distributor my size who signed real orders from your meetings.
- If the 15 free targets include buyers I genuinely could not have found myself, ideally with someone already booked for a trade fair I'm going to, I would run the pilot because the first 3 meetings cost nothing.
- If the free list of 15 companies had real buyers I did not already know, and they booked me 3-4 meetings at my stand before the next fair, I would try the pilot.
- If the free list of 15 companies came back with names I already wanted but could not reach, and I could speak to a wholesale or distribution client who got real orders from the meetings, I could take that to my partner and sign the pilot.
- Seeing my own SDR fail to book qualified meetings in the new country within a couple of months, or proof from a manufacturer like us (not just a logistics client) that the meetings turned into quote requests.
- A manufacturer or exporter like us, ideally one I know or can call, telling me their meetings turned into actual quotes and orders in the new market.
- If the free list of 15 companies included two or three I did not know and could see we should supply, and they could put me in touch with a manufacturer or exporter like us who closed real orders from meetings they booked.

Buyers say they would buy **1.0 times** in the first month on average.
